1. Preparing the RFM values

K-Means groups customers by the distance between them. Before clustering, the system (a) applies a logarithm to Frequency and Monetary, because a few very large values would otherwise stretch these two measures, and (b) standardizes Recency, Frequency and Monetary to the same scale (average 0, spread 1).

Why scaling is necessary: Monetary is in thousands, while Recency is in days and Frequency is a small count. Without scaling, Monetary would decide the groups almost alone and the other two measures would hardly matter.

2. Choosing the number of clusters

The system tried each number of clusters (k). Inertia shows how tightly customers sit around their cluster centres: the “elbow” is where adding clusters stops helping much. The silhouette score (-1 to 1) shows how clearly the clusters are separated: higher is better.
